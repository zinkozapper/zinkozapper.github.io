You shook hands, the price was right, and the deal was yours—until their legal team asked one question about your digital security that you couldn't answer.

You didn't lose the customer because of your product; you lost them because you looked like a risk. In today's market, trust now depends on your security.

Cybersecurity is more than another expense, it is a revenue enabler. If your company is not trusted then you are losing business. Trust can be just as important as your price point.

> Trust is no longer a soft issue; it's a business-critical asset... The companies that build it in by design will be the ones that customers choose.
> — Roger Roberts, Partner at QuantumBlack, AI<sup id="fnref1"><a href="#fn1">1</a></sup>

Your cybersecurity posture is what signals trust. Strengthening cybersecurity is a necessary investment for your company's survival.

To demonstrate, you'll see how strong cybersecurity practices can increase revenue, prevent costly incidents, and offer practical, actionable steps that you as a business owner can implement immediately.

### Generating Revenue

There is some evidence in the industry that cybersecurity investments directly contribute to company revenue. The enterprise hardware company Cisco polled companies on their estimated returns from privacy (security) investments (see Figure 1).<sup id="fnref2"><a href="#fn2">2</a></sup>

![Figure 1](cisco-privacy-benchmark.png)

**Figure 1:**
Source<sup id="fnref2"><a href="#fn2">2</a></sup>

Over 80% of companies found investing in security worth it. These returns are in part explainable by reduced downtime and increased customer retention.

Likewise, cybersecurity investment can assist in customer acquisition. A report from Gartner shows that 48% of companies prioritize security when deciding what software vendors to work with.<sup id="fnref3"><a href="#fn3">3</a></sup> Many large organizations now maintain security expectations as a prerequisite for any vendor relationship.

By meeting these expectations, your company becomes eligible for opportunities that competitors aren't eligible for. These opportunities include long-term contracts, new markets to expand into, and increased customer retention. All of these lead to an increase in revenue generation.

However, in order for you to reap the aforementioned benefits of security your customers need to know that your security exists. As economic researcher Toni Ahernt noted in a study on security spending, "Clients are willing to pay for security that they observe."<sup id="fnref4"><a href="#fn4">4</a></sup>

If your customers cannot see the security measures you have implemented, those measures may as well not exist from their perspective. Clear communication and demonstrable actions signal to customers that you take their protection seriously. Visible assurances build trust and reduce perceived risk.

The customer in the opening story failed to observe your security. Failing to invest in visible cybersecurity can have a dramatic impact on your company's success.

### Consequences of Failure

Cyberthreats are real and have financial consequences. The notion that your business won't be attacked because it's small doesn't hold true anymore. Verizon's 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report illustrates how frequently businesses of all sizes are targeted (see Figure 2).<sup id="fnref5"><a href="#fn5">5</a></sup>

![Figure 2](verizon-dbir.png)

**Figure 2:**
Source<sup id="fnref5"><a href="#fn5">5</a></sup>

Small companies (less than 1000 employees) are often targeted precisely because of their size and comparatively weaker defenses. Attackers don't just target specific small companies, they use automation to attack many companies at once.

This raises the question, how impactful are data breaches on your company's finances? A report from IBM notes that the cost of a breach in 2024 was $4.88M.<sup id="fnref6"><a href="#fn6">6</a></sup> This cost arises from factors including service downtime, service recovery, and legal fees.

Beyond the immediate legal costs, there is also potential for a lawsuit to be filed against your company. For your small business, incurring an expense of this magnitude would certainly be dire.

There are other factors that hurt your company in the wake of a breach. Breaches leave reputational scars on your company. Often it is harder to win back customers after suffering a breach than it was to gain them in the first place.

Showcasing this, Statista shows that less than 50% of American adults are likely to share their personal information with a company who has suffered a data breach in the past.<sup id="fnref7"><a href="#fn7">7</a></sup> This loss of confidence directly affects a company's ability to attract new customers and retain existing ones.

Because the financial risks are so high and customer expectations so clear, you must adopt immediate protections. Fortunately, there are simple actions you can take that will immensely lower your risk without incurring steep costs.

### Actionable Steps

One of the most financially damaging threats small businesses face today is ransomware. This is code that locks up all of your data; you can't access it until you pay a ransom.

Oftentimes this ransom will be hundreds of thousands of dollars. Company data often represents years of work. This causes companies to give in and pay the ransom in order to get their files back. However, if you can restore your data independently attackers lose this leverage.

The way to do this is by implementing a backup system. If something is worth saving, it's worth backing up. If you get hacked, you can rest easier knowing that you have copies of your vital data. In his book *Cyber Defense*, Edwards asserts an effective way to maintain backups is to use the 3-2-1 rule.<sup id="fnref8"><a href="#fn8">8</a></sup>

This strategy involves keeping three copies of your data on two different types of media, with one copy stored offsite. For example, your company could have one copy on their main disk drive, a second on a USB drive, and a third using cloud backup. This would satisfy all of the conditions of the 3-2-1 backup rule.

![3-2-1 backup rule](backup-321.jpg)

Source<sup id="fnref9"><a href="#fn9">9</a></sup>

Before hackers can ransom your data they get into your network. One of the most common methods they do this is through default credentials. Any internet-connected device (routers, cameras, or smart devices) comes with pre-set usernames and passwords. These credentials are widely available online and can allow attackers to access your devices.<sup id="fnref8"><a href="#fn8">8</a></sup>

In his book, *Cyber Security: The Complete Guide to Cyber Threats and Protection*, Sutton states that simply changing the default passwords on your devices significantly reduces your risk.<sup id="fnref10"><a href="#fn10">10</a></sup>

The final cost-effective and actionable step to take is to enable multi-factor authentication.<sup id="fnref8"><a href="#fn8">8</a></sup> Multi-factor authentication protects your accounts by making you verify your identity at least two different ways. If possible, you should use an app for multi-factor authentication as apps are significantly more secure than things like text message or email.

![Multi-factor authentication](mfa.jpg)

Source<sup id="fnref11"><a href="#fn11">11</a></sup>

This is an extremely effective safeguard to keep your accounts secure. This prevents people from signing into your accounts if they figure out your password. Anything you connect to over the internet should have this on. While it may feel inconvenient, a small annoyance is well worth it to prevent a major disaster.

These steps are not an all-inclusive list of everything you should do to secure your company. Instead, they are easy, actionable measures you can implement today. These actions signal to customers that you take their security seriously. In the opening story, if your business had taken these steps the conversation with the customer's legal team would have ended in confidence instead of lost revenue.

### Conclusion

As a small business owner, your business simply cannot survive without cybersecurity. This article has shown how cybersecurity decreases risk and long-term costs while increasing revenue. By taking the aforementioned simple actions of creating backups, changing default credentials, and turning on multi-factor authentication, you ensure that the next time a customer asks about your security, your answer strengthens the deal instead of ending it.
